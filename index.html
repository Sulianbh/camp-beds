Each row is a person: the calculation starts from this list. Name, rotation and crew are required. Blank dates are normal: add one only for someone who joins the project later or leaves it for good.

How to use it

Three things to fill in, in this order. The result updates with every entry.

  1. 1
    The camp

    The number of beds, those out of service, the possible extra beds, the safety margin, the reserved beds.

  2. 2
    The rotations

    For each rotation: days on site, days off site, arrival dates of the crews.

  3. 3
    The staff

    One row per person. The quickest way: “Paste from Excel”, then paste the rows or choose the Excel file. The two dates stay blank for most people: they follow their crew’s rotation.

Reading the result

  • Overview. Three figures: the beds in service, the demand on the busiest night, and the gap. Click a night on the chart: the bed board shows the camp that night.
  • Scenarios. Each row gives the effect, in beds, of an assumption or a decision. “That’s our case” or “Apply” puts it in place; “Undo” reverts it.

Good to know

  • Your data is saved automatically in this browser: it is still there when you reopen the page.
  • To use it on another computer or pass it on: File menu, “Save my data to a file”. A colleague opens it with “Open a data file”.
  • A bed is never used by two people on the same night. An overlap night, when the crew leaving and the crew arriving are both on site, therefore needs extra beds.
  • An incomplete staff row is still counted; it is flagged in red.
